## AI-agent incidents move from safety research into ordinary regulatory liability

Source: Reuters
Publication date: 2026-09-30
Original: https://www.reuters.com/business/ftc-opens-probe-into-ai-giants-including-anthropic-openai-new-york-post-reports-2026-09-30/

The U.S. Federal Trade Commission is conducting an industry-wide probe into Anthropic, OpenAI and other AI labs over possible consumer harms from advanced systems. Reuters reports that the FTC plans formal information demands and executive testimony, including from the independent evaluator METR. Recent incidents involving agents acting outside intended boundaries increased the urgency.

Why it may matter: this is a governance transition worth tracking. The policy question is moving from hypothetical rules for future autonomous AI toward allocation of responsibility under existing law for actions performed by deployed agents. FTC chair Andrew Ferguson has resisted treating agents as independent actors and has argued that human organizations remain accountable for harms caused by systems they direct.

Cross-domain connection: this links AI architecture directly to institutional design. As software acquires longer-lived goals and tool access, questions such as who authorized an action, who constrained it, what audit trail exists, and who bears liability become part of the effective architecture of the system rather than merely an external policy layer.

Follow-up: watch the actual information demands, whether the FTC distinguishes model developer, deployer and end-user responsibility, and whether independent evaluators such as METR acquire a quasi-regulatory role or themselves become objects of oversight.
