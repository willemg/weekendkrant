## Closed-loop AI moves from proposing science to testing hypotheses in a physical laboratory

Source: Euronews, reporting on peer-reviewed work from Chalmers University of Technology
Publication date: 2026-10-02
Original reporting: https://www.euronews.com/2026/10/02/swedish-researchers-create-an-ai-scientist-that-designs-and-runs-its-own-experiments
Primary source: https://www.chalmers.se/en/current/news/ai-scientist-autonomously-generates-and-validates-new-biological-discoveries/
Study: Agentic AI integrated with scientific knowledge: laboratory validation in systems biology, Journal of the Royal Society Interface

Researchers at Chalmers University of Technology, the University of Gothenburg and the University of Cambridge connected large language models and automated reasoning to the robot scientist Eve in a closed experimental loop. Working on Saccharomyces cerevisiae, the system was given genomic, metabolic and literature knowledge, generated candidate biological questions and hypotheses, selected experiments, interpreted the resulting measurements and used those results to refine subsequent reasoning. Follow-up reporting says the system generated 1,933 candidate hypotheses and experimentally identified underexplored interactions, including partial protection by aminoadipate against formic-acid stress.

Why it may matter: this is a stronger structural signal than an AI system that merely writes hypotheses or runs simulations. The epistemic loop crosses the boundary into physical measurement: machine reasoning changes the next experiment, reality supplies the error signal, and the system iterates. That makes autonomous laboratory infrastructure potentially a new scientific instrument rather than only a productivity tool.

Caution: the laboratory was not fully autonomous. Humans still performed some physical operations and defined the research boundaries, and the demonstration is in one well-characterized model organism. Claims of general autonomous science would therefore be premature.

Cross-domain connection: this directly couples topic 2 and topic 3. It also strengthens the methodological line already visible in this week's synthesis-route ingress: AI is increasingly being inserted into the experimental search loop itself, with physical or physics-based validation constraining model proposals.

Follow-up: independent replication; performance on less-characterized biological systems; comparison against expert-designed experiment sequences; how often generated hypotheses are genuinely novel rather than rediscoveries; and whether closed-loop systems converge on reproducible discoveries across laboratories.
